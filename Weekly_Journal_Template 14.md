# Week 14: Turning Data Into Stories

**Date:** April 17  
**Phase:** Visualizing the Narrative  
**Approach Used:** Troubleshooter

## Your Reflection

This week was one of the most demanding assignments so far because it combined both technical coding in R and interpreting what the results actually mean. Chapter 14 focused on describing data through visualizations, which sounded straightforward at first, but building clean, readable, and correct graphs in ggplot2 took a lot more effort than expected.

The biggest challenge for me was getting the visualizations to actually run without errors. Even small issues with factor ordering, missing values, or incorrect mappings would break the graphs or produce outputs that didn’t make sense. I spent a lot of time troubleshooting functions like `fct_reorder()`, `pivot_wider()`, and adjusting `position = "fill"` to get the stacked bar chart to display properly. At several points, I had to step back and do additional research or ask for clarification from my professor just to understand why certain plots weren’t rendering correctly.

Another difficult part was making the visuals “publication ready.” It wasn’t just about making a chart work, it had to be clear, labeled correctly, and visually meaningful. Choosing colorblind-friendly palettes and adding proper titles, subtitles, and captions forced me to think more like a communicator than just a coder. The interpretation section also pushed me further because I had to translate graphs into plain language and explain what the patterns actually meant, not just describe what I saw.

Even though this assignment was frustrating at times, it was also rewarding once everything finally worked. Seeing the final visuals come together made the earlier struggles feel worth it. It also helped me understand how data visualization is not just about aesthetics, but about telling a clear and accurate story from the data.

Overall, this week taught me patience, troubleshooting skills, and how much iteration is actually involved in producing clean, professional research visuals.
