# Week 11: Testing Before the Real Work

**Date:** March 23  
**Phase:** Analyst  
**Approach Used:** Troubleshooter

## Your Reflection

This week was definitely one of the more challenging ones, but also one of the most important. Chapters 11 and 12 focused on the pilot test and sampling plan, which forced me to slow down and really think through my research process before jumping into full data collection. At first, I underestimated how detailed a sampling plan needed to be. Writing out exact search terms, defining a clear date range, and explaining a sampling strategy took more effort than I expected because every decision had to be justified and replicable.

The most difficult part for me was the pilot test. Coding 10 items sounds simple, but once I actually started applying my codebook, I realized there were gaps and unclear categories. There were moments where I wasn’t sure which category to assign, especially when content didn’t fit neatly into one option. This made me go back and refine my decision rules, adding clearer definitions and sometimes even adjusting categories to better reflect real-world data.

Another challenge was making sure my sampling strategy was realistic. I had to think about how to narrow down large search results into a manageable sample size without introducing bias. Figuring out a systematic approach helped me feel more confident that my dataset would be consistent.

Even though it wasn’t the easiest assignment, I feel like I made it work. The troubleshooting process actually helped me understand why researchers emphasize pilot testing so much, it saves time and prevents bigger issues later. Overall, this week helped me build a stronger foundation for the next phase, especially as we move into full data collection and data wrangling.
