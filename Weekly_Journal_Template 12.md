# Week 12: Ethics Behind the Data

**Date:** April 3  
**Phase:** Field Work  
**Approach Used:** Connector

## Your Reflection

This week was more straightforward compared to the last, but it still felt important in a different way. Instead of focusing on technical skills like sampling or coding, we worked on completing the CITI Certification for ethical research. At first, it seemed like just another requirement to check off, but as I went through the modules, I realized how much it connects to the work I’m doing in this class.

The training covered topics like informed consent, privacy, and confidentiality, which made me think more deeply about my own project. Even though I’m working with publicly available data, the course made it clear that “public” doesn’t always mean ethically simple. For example, if someone posts something online, it doesn’t necessarily mean they expect it to be analyzed in a research study. That made me reflect on how I handle sensitive or personal content in my dataset.

This also connected back to last week’s pilot test. While I was focused on whether my codebook worked, I wasn’t really thinking about the ethical side of the data I was coding. Now I see that both parts matter, having a solid method and also making sure I’m treating the data responsibly. The idea of protecting individuals, even in indirect research like content analysis, adds another layer of responsibility to the project.

Overall, this week reinforced that research isn’t just about collecting and analyzing data, it’s also about doing it the right way. Earning the certification feels like more than just completing an assignment; it’s something I can actually use in the future, whether for internships, research opportunities, or graduate school.
